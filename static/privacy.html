NeuroLens privacy policy

Last updated: 26 October 2026

NeuroLens is an app that predicts the brain response to a video and shows the main brain response chart, the strongest and weakest moments, and a 3D brain map. It is free to use at the moment. Videos that people choose to share for research may later train NeuroLens's own models, which may become a paid product — that part is described under "Sharing a video for research" below. This page says what data NeuroLens handles.

What we collect

  • Your email address, from Google or from the address you sign up with. We use it only to recognise your account when you sign in and when you come back.
  • Videos you upload, and the analysis results made from them, including the 3D brain data.
  • A job record for each upload: the file name, when it ran, how long the video was and what it cost in credit.
  • A small picture from each video (its thumbnail, shown in History). It is kept with the result and is not part of any research copy.
  • Your sharing choice for each upload: whether you agreed to share it for research (below). The box is unticked by default and the analysis works either way.
  • A real audience-retention curve, if you choose to add one to a video you share, together with the few details the form asks for (rough views, when it was published, whether it was promoted with ads).

The 3D brain data is part of the analysis result. Deleting a video removes it.

We do not receive your Google password or your contacts. If you sign in with Google, we receive only your email address and whether Google has verified it.

How long we keep it

  • Uploaded videos that are not shared for research are deleted automatically after 30 days (up to 31 days, since deletion happens overnight). They are deleted at once when the job is cancelled or refunded.
  • Results are deleted automatically after 30 days.
  • A video you share for research, and any retention curve you add, are kept for up to 3 years. If no curve is added, the research copy is deleted 90 days after upload.
  • Your email address and job records stay until you delete your account, or until the project is taken down.

Sharing a video for research

Each upload has a separate, optional box: "Share for research and to train NeuroLens's own models. I own the rights to it." Sharing is never required and changes nothing about the analysis — the same prediction runs either way.

If you share a video, we keep the video itself, its file details, and any audience-retention curve you add with the details given alongside it. We use them to study how what is on screen relates to how audiences watch, and to build and test NeuroLens's own engagement models, which may later be offered as a paid product — with notice first. The models are trained on videos and real audience data, never on NeuroLens's brain predictions. Nothing is published, sold or given outside the team; anything shown publicly is only statistics. Everything is stored with Amazon Web Services in the United States.

The research copy is kept for up to 3 years, or deleted 90 days after upload if no curve was added. "Stop sharing" in the video's menu, deleting the video, or deleting your account removes the research copy at once. A model trained before that is not retrained, but your data is not used again.

By sharing, you confirm that you own the rights to the video and that any identifiable people in it agreed to appear. You keep all rights to the video and give us a free, non-exclusive licence to use it for the purposes above. As a thank-you, adding a valid audience-retention curve makes that video's analysis free.

AI reports

When you ask for a report, the video's frames (one per second), its spoken words and its brain numbers are sent to Claude on Amazon Bedrock in AWS us-east-1. AWS states that Bedrock does not store them or use them for training. The frames and the report are deleted with the job, and expire with the result after 30 days.

Who can see it

Only you, and the NeuroLens team, can see your videos and results. We do not sell or share them, and we do not use them for advertising. Sign-in is handled by Amazon Cognito.

Deleting your data

You can delete any analysis from your history on the main page: this removes its video, result (including the 3D brain data) and any research copy. You can also delete your whole account there by typing DELETE to confirm; this removes every video, analysis and row we hold, and your NeuroLens login. If a video is still queued or processing, wait for it to finish, then try deleting your account again. To ask what we hold about you, email joshualumzy@gmail.com.
